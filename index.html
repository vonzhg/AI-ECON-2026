AI for Economic Research: Dynamic Models, Language, and Agents

Zhigang Feng  ·  Professor of Economics

Webpage: https://sites.google.com/site/zfeng202/

A ten-lecture course — about twenty-four hours of material — on using AI as a working instrument of economic research: solving high-dimensional dynamic models with deep learning and reinforcement learning, turning economic text into measurement with language models, and running a research pipeline with agentic AI.

Everything is here and self-contained: the full syllabus, the lecture decks, and lab notebooks you can run in the cloud or on your own machine. The material is organized in six modules rather than calendar dates, so it works either as a taught course or as self-study at whatever pace suits you.

Start here

Three steps per module

  1. Read the module in the syllabus — it lists what each lecture covers and which lab goes with it.
  2. Download the topic decks from the slides page and work through them.
  3. Run the lab. The notebooks are the point where the material becomes yours — every one is offline-friendly and CPU-only.

Where to enter. With no Python background, start at Module 1 and do the setup lab first. With classical computational training already in hand, Modules 3–4 (deep learning and RL for dynamic models) are the bridge you are looking for. Interested only in text and agents, Modules 5–6 stand on their own. When you want to build something, go to the project tracks.

Where this course sits

The classical side of computational economics — value function iteration, time iteration, EGM, perturbation, projection methods, and high-performance computing — is covered in companion video lecture series (free) from 2025 and 2026, and in the textbook 机器学习与数量宏观经济学. Together they form one arc:

Classical Methods → ML & Deep Learning → Agentic AI Research

You can enter at any stage. This site is the third.
